# Active Perception Benchmarks — Landscape Review

*Compiled September 2026. Scoped toward indoor active mapping, semantic scene understanding, and budget-constrained object search, with an eye to nano-drone deployment.*

---

## How to read this

"Active perception" is not one benchmark community. It's at least six, and they don't cite each other much. The clusters below differ in what they actually score:

| Cluster | Scores | Maturity |
|---|---|---|
| Active mapping / exploration | Geometric surface coverage | Mature, large-scale |
| Active EQA | Answer correctness (± evidence consistency) | Mature, crowded |
| Next-best-view | Object-level reconstruction completeness | Mature, narrow |
| Change & lifelong | Relocalization / sequential search success | Fragmented |
| Informative path planning | Uncertainty reduction under budget | **No standard benchmark** |
| Active SLAM | — | **No standard benchmark** |

The recurring structural fact: **nothing scores the quality of the semantic map an agent builds while exploring.** Coverage benchmarks ignore semantics; EQA benchmarks treat the map as invisible instrumentation. That is the gap.

## 1. Active mapping and exploration

### GLEAM-Bench — ICCV 2025 · [arXiv 2505.20294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20294) · [github.com/zjwzcx/GLEAM](https://github.com/zjwzcx/GLEAM)

The current best-in-class option. 1,152 scenes drawn from ProcTHOR, HSSD (synthetic) and Gibson, Matterport3D (real-scan), curated for near-watertight geometry, ≥10 floorplan types, and complex interconnectivity. Connected to Isaac Gym for parallel simulation at ~150 FPS on an RTX 3090, trainable on 512 scenes simultaneously. Split: 1,024 train / 128 eval.

**What's interesting:** the motivating negative result. They trained ANS and OccAnt on 32 twelve-room scenes; the models performed well on 10-room scenes but *failed on simpler 5-room ones*. This retroactively undermines most pre-2025 active mapping numbers, which were trained and evaluated on 13–20 scenes. Any comparison against pre-GLEAM baselines needs this caveat.

**Release contents:** meshes (`obj/`), ground-truth surface points (`gt/`), asset indexing files (`urdf/`).

**Relevance:** highest. Their scene curation is reusable as a "suitable for an exploring agent" filter even if you don't use their task or metrics.

---

### AiMDoom / NextBestPath — ICLR 2025 · [arXiv 2502.05378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05378) · [shiyao-li.github.io/nbp](https://shiyao-li.github.io/nbp/)

A map generator built on the Doom engine, explicitly created because existing indoor datasets have limited geometric complexity and inaccurate ground-truth meshes. Four difficulty tiers (Simple / Normal / Hard / Insane) by scene size and connectivity, 100 scenes each. The NBP method predicts long-term goals rather than myopic next-views.

**What's interesting:** the complexity knob. Most benchmarks give you a fixed difficulty distribution; AiMDoom lets you sweep it, which turns "does this policy generalise" into a measurable curve rather than a binary. Exact GT surfaces, since scenes are generated rather than scanned.

**Trade-off:** visually unrealistic. Fine for planning-algorithm claims, useless for anything involving a real perception frontend.

---

### MAGICIAN — CVPR 2026

Long-horizon active mapping via "Imagined Gaussians" — a predictive 3DGS representation permitting fast coverage-gain evaluation for novel viewpoints inside a tree search. Reports results across indoor and outdoor benchmarks with varying action spaces.

**What's interesting:** the argument that long-horizon planning beats greedy NBV is now being made with a rendering-based gain estimator rather than an occupancy grid. Relevant if you're considering feed-forward reconstruction in the planning loop.

---

### Legacy baselines: ANS, OccAnt, UPEN, NARUTO

Active Neural SLAM (Chaplot et al., ICLR 2020) and Occupancy Anticipation remain the reference baselines everyone reports against. **Read their numbers with the GLEAM caveat above** — they were trained on 20–72 scenes and evaluated on 13–20.

## 2. Active embodied question answering

### OpenEQA — A-EQA split (Majumdar et al., 2024)

The de facto standard for active semantic evidence gathering. Common evaluation subset: **184 questions across 54 indoor scenes** from HM3D. Six categories: object recognition, attribute recognition, state recognition, object localization, spatial reasoning, functional reasoning. No predefined navigation goal — the agent designs its own exploration strategy. LLM-based answer scoring.

**What's interesting:** it's genuinely open-vocabulary and human-authored, unlike the rule-based question generation of EQA-v1 / MP3D-EQA / MT-EQA.

**Weakness:** answer correctness doesn't verify the agent actually observed the evidence. A model with strong priors can answer "is there a refrigerator in the kitchen" without looking.

---

### EXPRESS-Bench — [arXiv 2503.11117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11117)

Directly targets the weakness above. Introduces the **Exploration-Answer Consistency (EAC)** metric, which scores alignment between what was explored and what was answered. Also proposes Fine-EQA, a hybrid frontier-based / goal-oriented exploration baseline.

**What's interesting:** EAC is the metric to use if you want to claim your planner is genuinely information-seeking rather than benefiting from language priors. This is the single most reviewer-proof metric in the EQA cluster.

---

### E3VS-Bench — [arXiv 2604.17969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17969)

Embodied 3D Visual Search: **99 scenes reconstructed with 3D Gaussian Splatting, 2,014 question-driven episodes**, agents control viewpoint in full **5-DoF**. Built on the observation that existing EQA benchmarks rely on static observation or constrained egocentric motion and cannot evaluate viewpoint-dependent phenomena — e.g. an attribute readable only from one specific angle.

**What's interesting, and why this matters most for aerial work:** every other indoor benchmark in this document assumes a ground robot constrained to the floor plane. E3VS is the only one whose action space matches a free-flying platform. It also demonstrates that 3DGS reconstruction of real spaces is a viable substrate for a free-viewpoint benchmark — a useful precedent if you build your own.

---

### Others in this cluster

- **HM-EQA** (Explore-EQA) and **MT-HM3D** — additional HM3D-based EQA splits, commonly reported alongside OpenEQA.
- **FAST-EQA** ([arXiv 2602.15813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15813)) — SOTA on HM-EQA and EXPRESS-Bench with emphasis on inference latency and memory. Relevant to the inference-efficiency thread.
- **ActiView** ([arXiv 2410.04659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04659)) — active perception for MLLMs without embodiment; restricts the perceptual field and requires the model to zoom/shift. 27 models evaluated.
- **"An Exam for Active Observers"** / ActiveVision ([arXiv 2607.16165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16165)) — 17 tasks across three cognitive axes. Human participants average **96.1%, roughly 9× the best model's accuracy**, and increased reasoning effort barely narrows the gap. Useful for calibrating what a VLM can be expected to do before you put it on a drone.
- **CityEQA** ([arXiv 2502.12532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12532)) — outdoor/urban EQA, hierarchical LLM agent.

## 3. Next-best-view / active reconstruction

### GenNBV — CVPR 2024 · [arXiv 2402.16174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16174)

RL-based NBV policy with multi-source state embedding (geometric + semantic + action). Benchmark built in Isaac Gym on Houses3K and OmniObject3D, testing cross-dataset generalisation to unseen buildings and object categories. Reports ~98% / ~97% coverage ratio on unseen building-scale objects.

### ObjView-Bench — [arXiv 2605.10707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10707)

Revisits difficulty calibration and deployment realism for object-centric view planning — the recognition that prior NBV benchmarks are too easy and too far from robot constraints.

### Neu-NBV (Jin, Chen, Rückin, Popović — IROS 2023)

Uncertainty estimation in image-based neural rendering for view selection. The standard uncertainty-driven baseline, and the connection point between the NBV and IPP literatures.

**Overall assessment of this cluster:** least useful for room-scale flight. Object-centric NBV assumes a hemisphere of candidate viewpoints around a bounded target, which doesn't transfer to exploring an unknown apartment. Worth knowing for citation, not for evaluation.

---

## 4. Change, lifelong operation, and search

*This is the cluster that matters most for a "map → change → search" task design, and it's the most fragmented.*

### 3RScan / RIO — ICCV 2019 · [arXiv 1908.06109](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06109)

**1,482 RGB-D scans of ~478 real indoor environments across multiple timesteps.** Dense instance-level semantic segmentation per scan, *plus* ground-truth associations for objects that changed in appearance or location between rescans, with symmetry-aware local alignment. Captured with a handheld Google Tango phone; poses via offline bundle adjustment on fisheye imagery.

**What's interesting:** it remains the only large-scale *real* record of how indoor scenes actually change over time. The empirical change distribution — which categories move, how far, how often, appearance vs. pose — is extractable and can be used to parameterise synthetic change generation.

**Critical limitation:** not navigable. Coverage is whatever a handheld operator happened to scan; there is no navmesh and novel views will be full of holes. **Use it as a change model, not as an environment.**

### RIO10 — [arXiv 2008.02004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02004)

A 10-scene subset of 3RScan repurposed for long-term camera relocalization, selected for high scan frequency (5–12 scans each) and change diversity. Contributes a framework for quantifying appearance, geometric, and semantic change separately.

**What's interesting:** the change taxonomy. If you need to report "change magnitude" as an axis, this is the existing vocabulary to adopt rather than invent.

### ReScene4D — [arXiv 2601.11508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11508)

Temporally consistent semantic instance segmentation on evolving indoor scenes, evaluated on 3RScan. Defines **t-mAP**, extending mAP to reward temporal identity consistency.

**What's interesting:** a metric for "did you keep track of the same object across time" already exists. Reuse rather than reinvent.

---

### AI2-THOR Rearrangement / RoomR — Weihs et al., CVPR 2021

Two-phase structure: a **Walkthrough Phase** in which the agent observes the goal configuration, then the scene is shuffled, then an **Unshuffle Phase** in which the agent restores it. Up to five objects change location or openness. 80 train rooms / 4,000 tasks, 20 val / 1,000, 20 test / 1,000.

**What's interesting, and why it's a novelty hazard:** this is already `map → change → act`, five years old, with a leaderboard. Any new benchmark with that structure will be read as a RoomR variant unless the difference is stated precisely.

**Where it differs from a search-under-budget task:** the walkthrough is free and complete (no mapping budget), the objective is restoration rather than search, the agent is a discrete-action ground robot, and change is guaranteed recoverable.

---

### GOAT-Bench — [arXiv 2404.06609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06609)

Multi-modal **lifelong** navigation on HM3D. Sequential targets specified by category name, language description, or goal image, in a continuous environment without resets. Val splits: 36 scenes × 10 episodes, 5–10 subtasks each (~2,600 subtasks). Metrics: SR and SPL, success within 1 m.

**What's interesting:** the headline finding is that methods with effective memory representations achieve *higher efficiency* than those without — i.e. it's explicitly designed so that reusing a prior map pays off. That's the search-efficiency axis, already with baselines and a protocol.

**What it lacks:** scenes are static. No change axis at all.

---

### Change-Robust Online Spatial-Semantic Topological Mapping — [arXiv 2605.02227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02227)

**Read this one carefully.** Real-robot experiments on relocalization *and* object goal navigation under controlled combinations of appearance change and object rearrangement. Representations for all methods are built from an **identical mapping trajectory** in each scene, deliberately so that success can be attributed to representation robustness rather than planning strategy.

**What's interesting:** they explicitly control away the mapping-strategy variable. That's their declared scope boundary — and therefore the cleanest available gap for work that treats mapping budget as the independent variable.

---

## 5. Informative path planning

**There is no standard benchmark.** The field runs on per-paper baselines: lawnmower/coverage, random, spiral, RIG-tree, CMA-ES.

- **Waterberry Farms** ([arXiv 2305.06243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06243)) — the only dedicated IPP benchmark located. Measures movement-policy / environment-estimator pairs. 2D agricultural field monitoring, so limited transfer to 3D indoor.
- **IA-TIGRIS** ([arXiv 2502.15961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15961)) — incremental adaptive sampling-based online IPP, with an open-source codebase, simulation environment and testing package explicitly released for community benchmarking. Closest thing to shared infrastructure. Hardware-validated on two UAV platforms.

**Implication:** framing work as AIPP means reviewers will ask what you compared against, and there is no good answer available off the shelf. This is both a hazard and an opening.

---

## 6. Active SLAM

Also no standard benchmark, and the field says so itself. The Placed et al. survey ([arXiv 2207.00254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00254)) notes that evaluation happens in customised simulators or on specialised hardware, that one-to-one comparisons between approaches are rarely made, and that there is a "dire need" for performance metrics beyond exploration time and coverage. Unchanged since 2023.

---

## 7. Aerial

*Almost all outdoor. The indoor aerial benchmark space is close to empty.*

### ActiveFly-Bench — [arXiv 2607.10180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10180)

Three hierarchical tasks: Aerial EQA (reasoning), Fine-grained Language-guided UAV Control (execution), and Observation Behavior Planning (bridging). **7-DoF action space** jointly modelling UAV body and camera gimbal. 10k FLUC trajectories, 2.6k QA pairs, from real and simulated environments — three outdoor scenarios and one indoor. Real UAV deployment demonstrated.

**What's interesting:** baselines are reported as VLM+VLA pairs (GPT-5.4 + π₀-0.5, Gemini-2.5-Pro + OpenVLA), so the model classes are directly comparable to a π₀ / OpenVLA / SmolVLA study. Their failure analysis finds VLMs plan well under distant-blur and peripheral-view conditions but poorly under occlusion and non-frontal orientation.

### FG-EQA / ScoutVLA — [arXiv 2606.14772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14772)

**40K+ simulated and 1K human-piloted real trajectories.** Each episode isolates the *evidence-seeking* phase: the target is already visible at onset but requires a sequence of active viewpoint refinements to answer correctly. Continuous 5-DoF viewpoint control via flow matching.

**What's interesting:** the cleanest separation of "active perception" from "navigation" in the literature. Most benchmarks conflate them; here, finding the object is trivial and the difficulty is entirely in viewpoint selection. Outdoor, but the task formulation is portable.

### IndoorUAV — [arXiv 2512.19024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19024)

First large-scale benchmark targeting UAV-based vision-language navigation in 3D **indoor** environments. 16,040 trajectories / 3.9K instructions / 1,075 scenes drawn from MP3D, Gibson, HM3D and Replica. 4-DoF.

**What's interesting:** it's the indoor aerial simulation substrate, and it demonstrates that the standard indoor scan datasets can be flown rather than driven. Task is navigation, not perception.

### BEDI — [arXiv 2505.18229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18229)

UAV embodied-agent benchmark spanning real–virtual and dynamic–static dimensions, critiquing prior work (OmniDrones, AeroVerse, EmbodiedCity) for simplified virtual environments and single-turn static-image evaluation.

## 8. Active manipulation (adjacent)

**ActiveManip-Bench / SaPaVe** — CVPR 2026 · [arXiv 2603.12193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12193). Built on Isaac Sim with a G1 humanoid and a head-mounted active camera. 12 tasks, 100 objects, 20 scenes. Accompanied by ActiveViewPose-200K (200k image-language-camera-movement pairs).

**What's interesting:** their framing claim is that the vast majority of simulation manipulation benchmarks are restricted to fixed camera viewpoints, so there is a "noticeable absence" of benchmarks for evaluating active perception. That's the same gap diagnosis arriving independently from the manipulation side — useful supporting citation.

## 9. Scene generation resources

For synthesising scenes or generating controlled change:

| System | Venue | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ProcTHOR | NeurIPS 2022 | 10k procedural houses; assets in proprietary format, accessible only via Habitat/Unity |
| Holodeck | CVPR 2024 · [arXiv 2312.09067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09067) | LLM-guided; can produce multiple variants of the same input with different assets and layouts |
| HSSD | 2023 | Ships semantic lexicon, per-scene semantic annotations with regions, rigid + articulated (URDF) assets |
| PhyScene | CVPR 2024 | Physics/interactivity constraints in a conditional diffusion model |
| Architect | NeurIPS 2024 | Hierarchical 2D inpainting for scene population |
| InfiniteWorld | 2024 | Holodeck-derived scene generation and editing; OWSMM benchmark |
| SAGE / SAGE-10k | CVPR 2026 | 10k scenes, 50 room types, 565K unique 3D objects, agentic with a physics critic for simulation-readiness |
| Infinigen Indoors | CVPR 2024 | Fully procedural, photorealistic |

**Methodological rule:** generate change at the **asset/pose level, never the pixel level.** Inpainting or video-diffusion synthesis of an "after" state destroys the ground truth of what moved and where, which is the entire measurement.

## 10. Gap analysis

Three gaps, in descending order of confidence:

**1. Semantic map quality is never the objective.** Coverage benchmarks (GLEAM, AiMDoom) score geometry. EQA benchmarks score answers. ConceptGraphs-style metrics — object finding rate, query success R@k — exist but are only ever applied to *passive replay* of Replica/ScanNet sequences, never to a trajectory the system chose. Combining GLEAM-Bench scenes with ConceptGraphs metrics under a distance budget is a standalone contribution.

**2. Mapping budget is never the independent variable.** RoomR gives a free complete walkthrough. arXiv 2605.02227 fixes the mapping trajectory by design. GOAT-Bench has no change. Nobody asks: *how much exploration should you buy, given the map will be stale when you need it?*

**3. Ground-truth poses everywhere.** Every benchmark listed assumes GT or near-GT localization, which deletes trajectory-length pose degradation as a failure mode. A planner optimised under GT poses will happily select long trajectories that break a feed-forward reconstruction frontend. Injecting a calibrated drift model is a cheap and genuine differentiator.

## 11. Repurposing assessment

For a `map (budgeted) → change → search (budgeted)` task:

| Substrate | Semantics | Change | Navigable | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HSSD via Habitat** | Ships natively | Move assets, exact GT | Yes | **Best target** |
| ProcTHOR | Native | Yes | Yes | Assets proprietary, awkward extraction |
| GLEAM-Bench meshes | Asset indexing only | No (baked) | Yes | Use as *curation filter*, go to source for semantics |
| RoomR / AI2-THOR | Full | Built-in shuffle | Yes | **Cheapest path to a first result**; only ~120 rooms |
| GOAT-Bench | Full | None | Yes | Borrow the goal-specification format |
| HM3D-Sem | Full | None (baked) | Yes | Static arm only |
| 3RScan | Full + change GT | Real | **No** | Change model, not environment |

**Key reframe:** for synthetic scenes you are not *adding* semantic annotations — you are recovering semantics that already exist upstream and were discarded during mesh conversion for geometric-coverage evaluation.

**Irreducible gap:** real-scan scenes *with* change. Gibson/MP3D/HM3D meshes are baked; 3RScan isn't navigable. This is the one thing requiring new collection.

Useful plumbing: SceneVerse released preprocessing scripts for 3RScan, MultiScan, ARKitScenes and Structured3D, plus an explicit HM3D object-ID → HM3D-semantics mapping. (MultiScan worth checking independently — the name suggests repeated scans of the same spaces.)

Note that base HSSD scene datasets exclude pickable "clutter" objects; those import separately from e.g. `ovmm_objects`. The small movable items are exactly the ones you'd relocate between phases, and they arrive as a separate controllable library rather than baked into the room.

## 12. Metric notes

- **Don't use SPL** for search under a stale partial map. It presumes a known shortest path to a single goal, which is ill-defined when the map may be wrong. It also makes the work look like a GOAT-Bench variant.
- **Report budget–success curves, not points.** The counterfactual grid — no map / stale map / oracle map, crossed with change magnitude — is the informative object.
- **Borrow existing metrics where they exist:** EAC (EXPRESS-Bench) for exploration-answer consistency, t-mAP (ReScene4D) for temporal instance identity, RIO10's taxonomy for change magnitude.
- **Visibility masking is mandatory** for real captures. A handheld GT scan sees objects a fixed forward-facing drone camera cannot. Score recall against the *observable* subset (ray-cast from the reachable flight volume) and report observable fraction separately as a room property.

## 14. Degeneracy check (do this first)

The `map → change → search` task is degenerate at both ends: with a perfect map and no change, search is trivial; with total change, the map is worthless and the task reduces to plain ObjectNav. All signal lives in the middle of the change spectrum.

**Before building anything:** take 5–10 HSSD or ProcTHOR scenes, sweep change magnitude in ~5 steps, and plot search cost for a "trust the map" agent against an "ignore the map" agent. If the curves don't cross with a meaningful gap between them, the framing has no room in it — and that's a week of work rather than a semester.
